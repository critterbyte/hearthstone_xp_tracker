# Getting more XP

Fair tips for more reward track XP per hour. They follow from the [XP formula](FORMULA.md). Statements are marked the same way as there:

- **Measured:** found by this project.
- **According to [n]:** from the sources at the end of this page.

The plugin's dashboard shows where your XP goes. The "Lost XP" tab is a good place to start (see [USAGE.md](USAGE.md#tabs)).

## Play fair

This page is about playing normally and efficiently. Bots, win trading, account sharing and other ways around the game rules break Blizzard's terms and can get your account banned. They are not covered here.

## Choose the mode

| Mode | Win | Loss |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ranked (Standard and Wild) | 470 XP/h | 336 XP/h |
| Casual, Tavern Brawl and Arena | 336 XP/h | 240 XP/h |

- **Measured:** Ranked pays 1.40 times as much as Casual, Tavern Brawl and Arena. A Casual win pays the same as a Ranked loss.
- **Measured:** Standard and Wild pay the same. Play the format you enjoy or win more in.
- **Measured:** a win pays 1.40 times a loss. Your win rate matters, so play a deck you win with.
- **According to [1]:** Battlegrounds gives about 300 XP per hour. Not measured by this project.

## Do not go AFK

- **Measured:** if you let your turn run out without any action, that turn **and** the opponent's next turn give no XP.
- If you have nothing to play, click **"End Turn"**. A turn without any action still counts if you end it yourself.
- If you play at least one card, attack or use your hero power, the turn counts even if the timer runs out.
- **Measured:** when the **opponent** goes AFK, you lose nothing. Their idle turns count fully for you.

## Mind the 60-second minimum

- **Measured:** games with less than 60 seconds of XP time give **no XP**, not even a little. This includes games where the opponent concedes early.
- When you concede right after the start, the game gives nothing.

## Conceding

- **Measured:** conceding costs nothing extra. You get the loss rate for the time played.
- **Measured:** a lost game still pays the loss rate while it runs, so playing on is not wasted time.
- **Measured:** every new game costs time without XP: about 1:07 from the end of one game to the first turn of the next (average of 10 runs), plus about 35 seconds of mulligan. Conceding pays off when the next game is likely to be better, for example when you would rather play a game you can win.

## Game length

- **Measured:** XP per second is constant. Playing more slowly does not raise XP per hour of game time; it only makes each game worth more.
- Longer games spend a smaller share of your time in the queue and the mulligan. Please do not stall on purpose, though. It is poor manners towards your opponent.
- **According to [2]:** XP time is capped at about 30 minutes per game. Time beyond that gives nothing.

## Quests give most of the XP

- **According to [3]:** "Outside of the very hardcore player, majority of XP comes from quests."
- **According to [4]:** daily quests give 900, 1,000 or 1,500 XP. Weekly quests give 1,750 XP, and the guaranteed Ranked weekly ("Win 5 games of Hearthstone's Ranked Mode") gives 2,500 XP.
- **According to [5]:** you can replace one daily quest and one weekly quest each day. Progress on a replaced quest is lost.
- One daily quest is worth about two to four hours of Ranked games. Pick decks and modes that finish your quests while you play.

## Tavern Pass

- **According to [6] and [7]:** the Tavern Pass gives a 10% XP boost at once, 15% at track level 20 and 20% at level 70, for the rest of the expansion.
- **According to [7]:** the boost applies to XP from any source.
- Not measured: whether the boost multiplies the game rates exactly. All measurements were made without it.

## Track size

- **According to [6]:** levels 1 to 100 of the reward track need 155,200 XP in total. Bonus levels go up to level 400 and give 50 gold each.
- **According to [7]:** the track resets with every new expansion.
